# Falsification and Error Checks

## Claim tests

For a factual claim, identify the source, scope, counterevidence, and whether the source actually supports the wording. For a mathematical claim, inspect quantifiers, edge cases, hidden regularity assumptions, and whether computation covers the theorem's domain. For a causal claim, ask about confounding, intervention, mechanism, and temporal order.

## Design tests

Compare against a simple baseline, an adversarial input, a null case, a resource-constrained case, and a plausible alternative. Define a failure threshold before looking at results.

## Common traps

Watch for confirmation bias, survivorship bias, selection effects, benchmark leakage, analogy overreach, false precision, confusing optimization with explanation, and treating a lack of observed failure as proof of safety or truth.

## Constructive critique

State what is promising, what is unsupported, the smallest repair, and the next decisive test. Critique the claim or design, not the person.
