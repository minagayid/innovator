# Peer-review criteria: research writing & academic papers

Attack the piece the way a skeptical reviewer at a serious venue would — someone whose job is to keep weak work out, not to be encouraging.

- **Research question / hypothesis clarity** — is it stated precisely enough to be right or wrong? A vague question can't be tested, and a paper that never pins down what it's actually claiming can't be properly attacked or defended.

- **Methodology validity** — is the design capable of answering the question? Check sample size and power, missing control conditions, confounds that aren't addressed, and whether the method matches the claim (e.g. a correlational design can't support a causal conclusion).

- **Statistical / reasoning soundness** — is the right test used for the data? Is statistical significance being treated as if it were the same thing as practical significance? Multiple comparisons without correction, p-hacking-shaped patterns, and effect sizes that don't match the strength of the claimed conclusion are all fair game.

- **Novelty vs. prior work** — does it cite the work it's actually building on, and does it overclaim what's new? A paper that doesn't engage with the closest prior work can't be trusted to know it's actually novel.

- **Evidence-claim alignment** — this is the single most common failure: do the results in the data/findings section actually support the conclusions in the abstract and discussion, or is there quiet overreach between what was measured and what's claimed?

- **Reproducibility** — is there enough detail (data, code, parameters, procedure) that an independent group could redo this? "Trust us" is not a method section.

- **Structure** — does it follow the conventions of the field (e.g. IMRAD), and do the transitions between sections actually build an argument, or do sections sit next to each other without connecting?

- **Figures and tables** — do they support the text or contradict it? Watch for mislabeled or missing axes, cherry-picked ranges, and figures that look more dramatic than the underlying numbers justify.

- **Limitations** — are they acknowledged honestly, with real consequences spelled out, or is there a token "future work" paragraph that doesn't admit anything actually weakens the conclusions?

- **Ethical / safety considerations** — where relevant: human or animal subjects handling, data privacy, dual-use potential. A paper that doesn't address an obvious ethical question a reviewer would raise is incomplete, not just imperfect.
