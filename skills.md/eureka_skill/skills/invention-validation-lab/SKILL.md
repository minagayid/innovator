# Invention Validation Lab

Use this skill as the kill-or-continue gate. The goal is not to make every idea sound plausible; it is to identify the cheapest test that separates a strong mechanism from an attractive story.

## Workflow

1. **Freeze the candidate.** Record the exact mechanism, intended use, baseline, claimed improvement, operating regime, assumptions, and evidence. Do not validate a moving target.
2. **Check the prior-art boundary.** Search the nearest mechanisms, combinations, patents, standards, products, and negative results. Report overlap and the remaining contribution without declaring legal novelty.
3. **Stress-test the claim.** Inspect definitions, scope, causal direction, dimensional and logical consistency, counterexamples, rival explanations, environmental conditions, and failure modes. Call `critical-creative-thinking` when the claim is ambiguous or high-stakes.
4. **Check feasibility.** Assess materials, energy, data, manufacturing, maintenance, cost drivers, lifecycle, safety, regulation, accessibility, and adoption friction. Separate technical possibility from deployability.
5. **Define a baseline.** Specify the simplest credible alternative and the metric, sample, duration, controls, and threshold required for a meaningful comparison.
6. **Audit evidence credibility.** For computational or measured evidence, record versioned source/data, reproducibility path, implementation verification, external validation status, uncertainty/sensitivity status, and any unavailable layer with a reason.
7. **Design the smallest decisive test.** Prefer a reversible simulation, derivation, bench test, ablation, replication, or user experiment. State what outcomes would kill, revise, or support the candidate and what evidence would reverse the decision.
8. **Rank and hand back.** Score mechanism clarity, evidence quality, reproducibility, VVUQ status, falsifiability, feasibility, safety, reversibility, learning value, and prior-art distance. Return a kill/continue/reframe decision with unresolved risks.

## Validation language

Use “supported,” “consistent with,” “contradicted,” “not yet tested,” or “unknown.” Reserve “proven,” “validated,” “safe,” “novel,” “patentable,” and “commercially viable” for the corresponding evidence or expert process. A simulation is “verified” only for the stated implementation checks and “validated” only against an external reference in the stated regime. A literature gap is not evidence that an invention is new.

## Output

Use [validation-dossier.md](templates/validation-dossier.md). Include candidate summary, evidence ledger, prior-art boundary, adversarial checks, feasibility and safety table, baseline, decisive test, decision, confidence, and next bounded action.
