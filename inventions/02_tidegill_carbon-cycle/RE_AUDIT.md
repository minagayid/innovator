# Re-audit — TIDEGILL

**Disposition:** Continue only as a matched contactor benchmark. Keep oxygen
recovery and direct-carbon electrolysis as independent, separately gated
research trains.

## Corrections required

The fish-gill analogy supports a transfer-area versus flow-resistance question;
it does not demonstrate gas–sorbent CO₂ capture performance. MOXIE's oxygen
recovery from CO₂ produces CO as the other product and does not validate
elemental-carbon production. Molten-carbonate carbon deposition is electrolyte
and condition dependent.

The legacy dossier's cathode equation
\(\mathrm{CO_2+4e^-\to C+O^{2-}}\) is oxygen-unbalanced. A generic oxide-ion
shuttle bookkeeping pair is
\(\mathrm{CO_2+4e^-\to C+2O^{2-}}\) and
\(\mathrm{2O^{2-}\to O_2+4e^-}\), with net
\(\mathrm{CO_2\to C+O_2}\). This is a balance check only, not a universal
mechanism for a specified molten-carbonate cell; use electrolyte-specific
reactions for any real process claim.

The current \(\Phi=\dot m/(P_{fan}+P_{regen})\) metric adds thermal heat and
electric power as if interchangeable and omits conditioning/CO₂ handling.
Report total electrical energy per captured mass and regeneration heat per
captured mass separately. Only combine them after defining a common, disclosed
primary-energy or exergy conversion and system boundary.

## Decisive test

Freeze one sorbent and cycle, then compare lamellar and conventional contactors
at matched feed, humidity, sorbent inventory, capture target, cycling, and
uncertainty. Include conditioning and CO₂ handling in the boundary. A positive
contactor result does not transfer to the independent carbon cell or establish
net carbon removal.
