# TIDEGILL Carbon-Cycle Research Programme

TIDEGILL is a gill-inspired **contactor-geometry hypothesis**, not a passive system that makes free carbon and oxygen from air. It uses a lamellar counterflow geometry to test an energy-normalized capture advantage and deliberately separates capture from oxygen recovery and experimental direct-carbon electrochemistry.

The first benchmark compares a TIDEGILL lamellar cassette with a conventional monolith under the same capture medium, feed, outlet target, pressure-drop limit, cycling schedule and calibrated fan/regeneration electricity measurement. The programme passes its first gate only if its energy-normalized capture rate remains better after degradation is included.

> MOXIE supports the principle of oxygen extraction from CO₂; it does not establish the separate claim of continuous battery-grade solid-carbon production. Carbon cell performance, material quality and lifecycle pathway are independent gates.
