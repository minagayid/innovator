# Multi-reviewer debate summary

Three independent reviewers challenged the initial broad “neuro-generative”
concept.

## Science reviewer

Argued that a universal platform covering Parkinson disease, spinal-cord injury,
stroke, and neurodegeneration would blur mechanisms and endpoints. Recommended
Parkinson V1 focused on dopaminergic cell-replacement research, with separate
evidence axes and explicit model-transfer limits.

## Translational-safety reviewer

Required a research-only intended-use lock, deterministic refusal behavior,
provenance and privacy fields, independent human review, preserved dissent, and
no patient-specific, gene-editing, dosing, manufacturing, transplantation, or
external-execution outputs.

## Repository architect

Recommended an additive numbered package with schemas, synthetic examples,
offline tests, a scope schema, and no model-training or wet-lab implementation
in the first release.

## Resolution

The panel rejected the universal chamber as a human delivery concept and kept it
only as a visualization metaphor. V1 is now a narrow Parkinson research twin;
spinal-cord injury and other modalities are future separately reviewed modules.
The strongest claim is a traceable, evidence-grounded, safety-gated research
operating system—not a regenerative therapy.
