# Re-Audit: MEL-SHIELD

**Method:** claim freeze → mechanism separation → spectrum audit → mass/thermal accounting → biosafety gate → decisive coupon test.

## Claim corrections

| Original framing | Audit finding | Revision decision |
|---|---|---|
| A fungus “eats” or ends radiation. | Ionizing radiation is not destroyed by fungal metabolism; energy is absorbed, scattered or converted to heat and electronic excitation. | **Reject the wording.** Use radiation management, attenuation and material interaction. |
| Melanin alone is a complete shield. | Shielding is spectrum-dependent, and hydrogen-rich, dense and neutron-management layers have different roles. | **Reject.** Use a multilayer stack and calibrated testing. |
| Radiation-associated growth proves useful power generation. | A changed redox signal or growth rate does not establish net electrical output. | **Reframe.** MEL-CONVERTER is a falsifiable, low-confidence branch. |
| Engineered living fungi should be deployed in the shield or environment. | Persistence, mutation, spores, containment and exposure pathways are not closed. | **Reject.** First articles are nonliving; living work is regulated and separate. |
| A successful simulation or dark coating validates the shield. | Simulation and appearance do not establish spectrum-specific attenuation, secondary radiation or aging. | **Reject.** Require matched calibrated coupons. |

## Retained contribution

The retained invention is a **bio-inspired radiation-management material platform** that uses fungal-melanin research to motivate a nonliving composite layer, then integrates it with conventional hydrogen-rich and neutron-management materials, sensors and thermal management.

## Prior-art boundary

Melanized-fungi radiation studies, NASA space-biology research and hydrogen-rich shielding research are prior art or adjacent mechanisms [1]–[4]. This package makes no legal novelty, patentability, safety certification or operational-use claim.

## Final decision

**Continue with material coupons and spectrum-specific measurement. Stop before human, spacecraft or nuclear-facility deployment until independent radiation, materials, thermal, mechanical and regulatory evidence exists.**

## Scientific review additions

The evidence includes an important negative result: tested melanin did not
improve β-radiation shielding relative to cellulose. A 2025 PNAS study provides
a narrow positive result for particular fungal-melanin/PLA material coupons
after roughly six months in LEO, including reduced darkening of a PVC backing.
Neither result supports dose-equivalent protection of people, cosmic-ray
qualification, or universal material performance. Add matched areal-mass
controls, declared spectrum and geometry, calibrated dose and secondary-
particle measurements, and aging to the first coupon gate.

If fungal melanin is produced from *Cryptococcus neoformans*, source-material
and worker-containment review is a separate upstream concern; CDC describes
infection risk from inhaled spores. This does not imply purified melanin is
infectious. The first deployed test article remains nonliving. NIH Guidelines
apply only to covered work within their funding/institutional scope; determine
applicable local oversight separately.

Sources: [2020 β-shielding comparison](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229921),
[2025 LEO PLA-composite study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2427118122), and
