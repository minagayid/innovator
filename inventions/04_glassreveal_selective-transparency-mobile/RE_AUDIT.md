# GlassReveal Re-Audit

## Original claim corrected

The original “transparent glass mobile phone” framing conflicts with opaque battery, logic, camera, thermal and RF requirements. It is therefore rejected as a whole-device claim.

## Retained mechanism

A conventional phone can use a rear normally scattering reveal laminate and intentionally composed opaque cassette to provide a controlled visual-reveal feature. The main display and safety-critical functions remain conventional.

## Rejected paths

| Rejected path | Reason |
|---|---|
| Transparent battery / transparent logic core | Does not meet handset energy/computing requirements in the proposed scope. |
| Transparent OLED as the primary handset display | Ambient contrast and system integration would make basic phone readability dependent on a high-risk layer. |
| Full-glass structural/RF/thermal architecture | Concentrates unrelated structural, thermal and radio risk in the visual concept. |

## Decisive falsifier

If a rear-laminate mule cannot show a repeatable useful reveal/private state without unacceptable haze, drift, heat/functional interference or service penalty, retain only a static clear perimeter or terminate the active reveal path.

## Unresolved

Film durability at handset thickness, functional isolation, repair cost, real-user value, manufacturing yield and product/regulatory qualification remain unresolved.

## Scientific review additions

Freeze one powered-clear PDLC formulation for Rev-A and state its off/on
polarity; do not group PDLC and PSLC as if they share a switching direction.
PSLC literature includes a transparent off-state that scatters when powered,
while PDLC reports the opposite mode. The source is stack-specific, not a
general transparent-phone result ([PSLC prototype](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54109-0),
[PDLC study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41378-024-00855-6)).

For a first coupon screen, pre-register provisional targets of at least 60%
clear-state visible transmission, at most 10% clear-state haze, at least 80%
reduction of printed-target contrast in private mode, no more than 5 ΔE00
colour shift, no more than 15% contrast variation over the declared viewing
angles, and no more than 10% optical drift after 10,000 switches. These are
proposed falsification targets, not sourced product standards; revise them
before testing if the intended visual use requires different values. Measure
switching latency and energy as separate outputs.
